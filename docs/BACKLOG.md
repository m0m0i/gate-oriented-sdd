## A note on how this list was built

`backlog` expects to read epics and hand shaped items to `sprint`, which then creates the issues. For the first three groomings that is not what happened: every item already existed as a tracker issue, filed as it was discovered while using the harness on itself, and this document imposed an order on work that was already committed. The fourth grooming named that inversion and was the first with epics to read.

This grooming inherits a third variant of the same problem, and it is the finding worth leading with: **between the fourth grooming and this one the list was not read at all.** Twenty-four issues closed and eighteen were filed between 2026-09-05 and 2026-09-17, and the document did not move. Every row that shipped, shipped without the list being told. What was actually driving the work is recorded below, because it is not this file.

Then it happened again, to this grooming, in the two days between writing it and merging it. That revision is the last bullet of the next section, and it is the most direct evidence in this document that #79 is a real defect rather than housekeeping.

## What changed at this grooming

- **The list stopped being the plan, and the work logs became it.** Rows 1, 2 and 14 of the fourth grooming were all discharged — #126 ran `init` (row 1), #47, #48 and #72 landed (row 2), and #22 closed via #110's mode work (row 14) — and none of it was recorded here. Meanwhile the eighteen issues those runs produced were worked straight out of each day's **Next steps** in arrival order, never re-entering this list. The fourth grooming's own phrase for the first version of this was "the tracker was the plan for a week". The tracker has been replaced by the work log; the inversion is unchanged.
- **The backlog did work as a generator, and that is the part to keep.** Three of its rows produced shipped work, and row 1's reasoning — that each run files three or four defects, and what they find outranks what is below — was right: #126's run filed #127 and #130, and both shipped inside a day. The ordering was sound. The bookkeeping between a run and the next grooming is what is missing.
- **#26 is displaced a fourth time, and this is the last grooming that is allowed to do it quietly.** Three groomings ranked it first or near-first and three times it lost to cheaper work. A row that loses four times is not being ordered, it is being wished for. It is row 2 here and the reason it is not row 1 is stated in its own cell rather than left to be re-argued.
- **#131 is new at row 1**, and it is the one genuinely new judgment in this grooming: the dogfood reviewer cannot run three of the validators the project requires, so every review below it is weaker than it reads — including the review of #26.
- **#22 closed from last place.** It sat at row 14 of 14 and was closed as completed on 2026-09-12 by work done for #110. Either the position was wrong, or it was a dependency of a higher row and the list never said so. Recorded as a mis-order rather than smoothed over.
- **The fourth grooming's row 13 — "re-run `epics` to home the issues filed since it ran" — did not happen, and the `Epic` column is now mostly empty.** Ten of eighteen rows carry `—`. It is last again, with a decision attached this time: if it loses a third grooming, the column should be deleted rather than kept as a field that is blank more often than not.
- **The `sprint` trigger is recorded as partly fired**, which `.work_logs/2026-09-05.md` asked of this grooming and #79 has not landed to do instead. See **Triggers that have already fired**.
- **This grooming went stale in two days, before it had merged, and is revised rather than reopened.** It was written on 2026-09-17 against 0.9.0; six commits landed on `main` before it merged, taking the plugin to 0.12.0. #133 shipped out of row 11, three issues arrived (#139, #140, #146), and the Antigravity `SessionStart` item was answered and left Unshaped — `main` had already deleted that bullet, which is the conflict this revision resolved. **That is the lead finding happening to this document while it was being written**, and it is the argument for #79 being real work rather than a tidy-up: nothing connects a merge to the list, so the list is stale by the time anyone reads it. Recorded here rather than silently rewritten, because a grooming that quietly absorbs two days of drift teaches the next reader that the dates mean nothing.
