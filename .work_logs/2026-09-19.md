### Decisions

- **The version becomes unable to be wrong rather than reliably caught.** Detection already existed and worked — CI caught the #141 drift at the PR. What it could not do is prevent, because the turn that changes the version is the turn `quality-gate.sh` is guaranteed to skip (#14 put the manifests outside `Source globs`). Removing the second source is the LV-1 argument applied to documentation.
- **The badge reads `main`'s manifest, not the latest release.** A release-derived badge would decouple the claim from `plugin.json`, so a manifest disagreeing with its release would be hidden by the badge rather than caught by it. That mismatch is #68's gap and stays #68's work.
- **ADR-5 and EPICS #48 are left as historical records.** Both carry a premise this change falsified. An ADR records what was decided and why at the time; amending it to stay true would erase the decision it exists to hold.

### What seven rounds actually found, and the lesson

Rounds 2, 3, 4 and 5 each found a defect **introduced by the previous round's fix**, and every one was the same species: a check that had quietly stopped being able to fail. A widened pattern with no case that reddens when reverted. A negative assertion matching a string the reword had deleted. A fixture that would degenerate into its own control if a heading moved. A narrowing that missed the likelier input. None was reachable by running the suite, because **a suite that cannot fail is green**.

The method that found them is the transferable part: after changing a guard, revert the change and confirm the suite goes red — and read the *whole* failure list, not one grepped line. That last clause is not a flourish. Round 3's record claimed a mutation reddened one half "and nothing else"; round 4 caught it, and re-running showed **six** reports red. The check had been real; the record of it was fiction, produced by grepping a single line out of a six-failure run. A wrong verification record is worse than none, because the two previous rounds had turned on exactly such a claim.
