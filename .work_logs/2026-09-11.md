### Decisions

- **Correct the description, not the flow — so 0.5.1, patch.** `#52`/`#113` would argue minor since
  `archive` moves from opt-in to mandatory, but nothing mechanical reads the set today: it is only
  ever described. `archive` was always mandatory and the document said otherwise. The behaviour
  change lands on **#110**, which is what makes the set machine-read, and which the author is
  holding the major for.
- **No new guard, and the reviewer attacked the argument and agreed.** The obvious guard — check the
  three files agree — *would have passed on this bug*: they agreed perfectly and rendered one
  confused sentence three times. A guard that cannot fail on the defect it is written for is itself
  the fail-open. #54 is the standing argument against growing the guard count.
- **Replace the check rather than patch it a third time.** After direction and markup blind spots, a
  fourth grep patch was the wrong move. The partition assertion — `seen == ALL and optin == OPT`,
  plus `mandatory()` observed directly rather than derived as `ALL - OPT` — is markup-aware and
  direction-free, and the reviewer could not reach a false green through the table parser, because
  an equality means a parser failure drops all four names at once and lands red.
- **Sentence-scoped matching cannot see anaphora, and that is recorded rather than chased.**
  「`archive` も同様です。」 defeats it and no amount of pattern-matching reaches it. Documented as a
  RESIDUAL naming exactly what the check cannot see, with #110 as the exit — the same disposal
  `.steering/product.md` already uses for the `CAP-`/#22 gap. Chasing it would have bought a
  seventh round for a string that exists nowhere.
- **Two LOWs accepted, recorded after the receipt** so `verify.py`'s logic is exactly what was
  reviewed: the working-tree path raises instead of returning `unreadable`, and the recorded
  invocation `./verify.py main:red` rots once this merges. Both fail closed. The remedy for the
  second is written down — pin the pre-branch SHA, `./verify.py 18205d5:red -:green`.
- **C-8 accepted, not repaired by rewriting history.** The AC1 amendment shares `a827e84` with the
  artifact it judges. The branch was unpushed and the commit could have been split, but C-8 protects
  the evidence trail, and rewriting history to erase a process defect from it is the move #102
  settled against. The reviewer independently re-ran AC1 at both refs in every round, which is the
  correct repair for a C-8 that has already happened.
- **`produces:` left English in the Japanese diagram, deliberately undecided.** Both READMEs stay

### Two lessons that transfer beyond this spec

- **A pattern copied between an accusing check and a clearing check must have its direction
  re-decided.** The same markup-tolerance gap appeared twice: in `NEG` a bolded negation produced a
  false red, in `NEG_MAND` a false green. Which side of `G-9` the pattern sits on decides the
  severity, not the gap.
- **An expectation that a check fails is not evidence the check works** unless *could not look* is
  distinguishable from *looked and found it*. Round 4 added `:red` expectations to make the check
  self-testing and thereby made an unresolvable ref satisfy them — `./verify.py mian:red` exited 0
  having read nothing. That is #16 reappearing at the outermost layer.
